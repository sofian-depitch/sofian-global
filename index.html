1:1 session

What happens in a session.

30 to 60 minutes per session, 1 to 3 sessions per startup. The times below are for a 60-minute session.

01
01

Live pitch review

10 min

The startup presents the pitch in real conditions. I time it, watch it the way an investor does, and note every point where the audience loses attention.

02
02

Pitch structure review

10 min

We put the slides in the right order for global investors. Context, problem and solution come first, and technology comes later. This is the opposite of a typical Korean deck. Weaker parts move to the appendix for Q&A.

03
03

Slide-by-slide feedback

20 min

Every claim gets a number, and the main message goes into the slide title. I check each number with the startup before an investor questions it. We rewrite weak sentences together.

04
04

Live Q&A practice

15 min

The startup answers hard investor questions. We improve the weakest answers first, then write and practice each answer until it takes less than 60 seconds. The hardest questions are answered on the slides, before anyone asks them.

05
05

Wrap-up and written report

5 min

The report shows what to change on each slide, with the exact new words. It also notes what works and should stay, and gives a short to-do list for the next pitch.

Global pitch deck standards

A global pitch deck follows different conventions than a Korean IR document.

  • One slide, one message
  • Open with context, problem, then solution
  • Show the wow before the technology
  • Put the slide's message and number in the title
  • Write with certainty, no "trying" or "maybe"
  • Plain words, no jargon or acronyms
  • Back every claim with a number
  • Show a market entry plan for the region you pitch
  • Traction on one slide: revenue and growth, not MOUs
  • Ask slide: amount, use of funds, milestones
  • Show all money in USD, not Korean won

FAQ

Common questions.

Will sessions be in English? Will Korean founders follow?
Yes. I have run 20+ seminars and coached 240+ Korean startups 1:1 in English, without language problems. Working in English is also practice for the real pitch, because investors abroad will not switch to Korean.
Why work with a non-Korean coach?
Korean startups already know how to pitch in Korea. The gap is what global investors look for, and that is hard to see from inside Korea. I have done business in Europe and Asia for 13+ years, and I work with international VCs every month.
How does invoicing work? Can you issue a Korean tax invoice (세금계산서)?
Contracts and invoices go through Carte Blanche SAS, my French company. I invoice your organization directly, with an international commercial invoice instead of a Korean tax invoice (세금계산서). Korean program operators already accept it. For government-funded programs, I also provide the administrative documents requested during the contract and project period, including documents in Korean.
Can I get a written quote?
Yes. After the call, I send a quote based on the number of startups and sessions, with the documents your contract needs.
Do you specialize in specific industries or stages?
No. I coach startups in any sector and at any stage up to Series B. I have coached medtech, deep tech, hardware, consumer brands, AI and gaming, food tech and B2B SaaS startups. The deck basics stay the same, and I adapt the angle to each startup's target market.
Can you deliver in person in Seoul, or only online?
Both. I come to Seoul 3 to 4 times a year for in-person sessions. Online sessions work equally well. I have coached 240+ Korean startups since 2022, in person and online.
